About the Study

A multicenter randomized clinical trial testing a novel approach to preserving lean body mass in patients treated with incretin-based obesity medications

Background

New incretin-based medications (such as Wegovy and Mounjaro) produce impressive weight loss. There is a catch, however: according to published studies, 25% to 40% of the total weight lost comes from lean body mass, and with it, a considerable loss of muscle mass.

Losing muscle mass can impair daily function, weaken the body, and raise the risk of a condition called sarcopenia: a progressive loss of muscle mass, strength and function, usually as part of aging, but also as a result of physical inactivity, inadequate nutrition or chronic disease.

What are we testing?

Can a daily protein supplement (25 g per day) combined with resistance training (3 times a week) help preserve lean body mass, even while taking incretin-based obesity medications?

The study is conducted at Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center (Ichilov) and at Assuta Ramat HaHayal Medical Center.

Why Does This Study Matter?

The problem, and the solution we are testing

New Drugs, a New Challenge

The new incretin-based obesity medications are highly effective for weight loss and for improving related conditions, but part of the weight lost comes from lean body mass, and with it, from muscle mass.

Muscle = Health

Muscle is essential for movement, stability, a healthy metabolism, and preventing falls and fractures, especially as we age.

A Science-Based Solution

We are testing whether combining a protein supplement with resistance training can reduce the loss of lean body mass and support higher-quality weight loss.

How Does the Study Work?

240 participants are randomly assigned to one of two groups

🔹 Control Group

  • Healthy eating recommendations

🟢 Intervention Group

  • Daily protein supplement: 25 g per day
  • Resistance training 3 times a week (one session via Zoom with a trainer)
  • Healthy eating recommendations

* The study does not provide the medication and does not advise on medication-related matters

Measurements and Assessments

A range of advanced measurements is carried out throughout the study to assess the effect of the intervention

Body Composition

  • Body composition and bone density by DXA *
  • Body composition by bioelectrical impedance (BIA) using InBody
  • Ultrasound of the anterior thigh muscle (rectus femoris) *

Anthropometric Measurements

  • Weight
  • Height
  • Circumferences (waist, calf)

Physical Function Tests

  • 2-minute walk test
  • Handgrip strength
  • Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB)
  • 30-second sit-to-stand test
  • Wearable sensors for physical function and balance *

Diet and Behavior Questionnaires

  • 3-day food diary
  • Food frequency questionnaire (FFQ)
  • Additional questionnaires

Nitrogen Balance

  • Urine collection to measure urinary nitrogen excretion

Metabolic Rate

Resting metabolic rate (RMR) measured by indirect calorimetry with the Q-NRG device

* Performed at Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center (Ichilov) only

Eligibility Criteria

Inclusion and exclusion criteria for the study

✅ Inclusion Criteria

  • Age 45 or older
  • BMI ≥ 30, or BMI ≥ 27 kg/m² with at least one adiposity-related complication
  • Able to read and speak Hebrew
  • Starting treatment with Wegovy or Mounjaro

❌ Exclusion Criteria

  • Contraindications to Mounjaro or Wegovy (e.g., trying to conceive, current pregnancy or breastfeeding)
  • Treatment with insulin or sulfonylureas
  • Uncontrolled psychiatric disorders
  • Diagnosis of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)
  • Cognitive impairment
  • Active bulimia nervosa
  • Past metabolic bariatric surgery or endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty
  • Use of obesity medications in the past month
  • Active weight-loss treatment with a loss of ≥5% of body weight in the past 3 months
  • Implanted electronic device (e.g., pacemaker)
  • Significant physical limitation that would prevent independent resistance training
  • Resistance training more than once a week during the past 3 months
  • Chronic kidney disease requiring dietary protein restriction
  • Avoidance of milk-based protein supplements for any reason (e.g., milk allergy, vegan diet)
  • Frequent use of protein supplements
